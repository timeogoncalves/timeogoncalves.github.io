Research

Productivity dynamics in Egyptian manufacturing, 2013–2020

Policy memo, LSE PP455 Quantitative Approaches and Policy Analysis

Did Egypt’s post-2013 reforms make its manufacturing firms more productive? Using three waves of the World Bank Enterprise Surveys (3,936 firm-year observations), the memo estimates firm-level total factor productivity with the Levinsohn–Petrin method, in gross-output and value-added specifications, and decomposes it following Olley and Pakes. Allocative efficiency improved markedly by 2020 as output shifted towards more productive firms, yet average firm productivity stagnated: the gains came from reallocation rather than from innovation within firms.

The memo closes with four priorities: broad-based diffusion of technology and managerial practices, stronger competition enforcement, regulatory simplification and formalisation, and investment in human capital and research.

Off-grid electrification and democratic practices in sub-Saharan Africa

Undergraduate thesis, ESPOL, 2025

Off-grid solutions now bring electricity to communities beyond the reach of national grids across sub-Saharan Africa. The thesis asks whether this access also strengthens democratic practices, using a geospatial event-study difference-in-differences design.

With a team of LSE consultants, I am designing two randomised experiments for the Bank of England: one on herding behaviour, and one comparing how different large language models affect financial stability on stability-sensitive tasks. I also draft the terms of reference and handle client meetings and scoping. The engagement ends with a final report to the Bank.

Powering public services in southern Syria

War-room simulation, LSE PP414 Policy-Making

How can hospitals, schools and water pumps in As-Suwayda and Daraa keep running while grid repairs take years? The options appraisal weighs the status quo and a diesel bridge against public-first renewable mini-grids, delivered under Syria’s 2016 public–private partnership law with donor co-financing and community oversight.

Clean Field: paddy-residue burning in Punjab

Group project, LSE PP425 Strategic Policymaking

A policy package to curb the burning of paddy residue in Punjab. My part covered the straw-collection franchise option and a spatial rule I proposed: farmers within 60–80 km of straw end-users join franchises, while those farther away receive a ₹2,500 subsidy.

Also at the LSE: the design of a statutory infrastructure levy for Tanzania’s mining sector.
